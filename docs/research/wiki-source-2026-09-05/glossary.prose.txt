Currencies & Resources
Coins
The main farming currency, earned from kills and wave bonuses during runs. Spent on workshop upgrades and labs — everything in the long game ultimately converts coins into permanent power.
Cash
The in-run currency. Earned during a single run and spent on temporary upgrades while you play; resets when the run ends.
Gems
Premium currency earned from ad chests, missions, events and "Bob". Spent on card draws, lab rushes, respecs and event stock.
Stones
The Ultimate Weapon currency — used to unlock UWs and buy their upgrades (and unlock card mastery / assist slots). Earned mainly from tournaments and events; long-term, stone investment into econ is the biggest multiplier on how fast your account grows.
Cells
Dropped by elite enemies (rays, vampires, scatters). Spent on lab speed-ups — the higher your farming tier, the faster your labs can run.
Shards
Module currency. Used together with coins to level modules; earned by shattering spare modules, daily missions and shops. Reroll shards are a separate type used to reroll substats.
Medals
Event currency invested into bots. Respeccing medals out of a bot costs gems, so plan the order.
Bits
Guild currency spent on guardian chip upgrades. Earned from guild mission chests and the guild store.
Bob
Community nickname for the floating gem that drifts across the screen during runs — tap it for bonus gems, up to a handful of times per run.
Ad gems
The small gem claim that appears on screen every few minutes. A vault upgrade lets claims stack so AFK farmers don't lose them.
Interest
An in-run mechanic that pays a percentage on your unspent cash — one of the reasons hoarding cash briefly can beat spending it instantly.
Number suffixes (q vs Q)
The game abbreviates big numbers case-sensitively: K, M, B, T, then q (quadrillion, 1e15), Q (quintillion, 1e18), s (sextillion, 1e21), S (septillion, 1e24). "Small q" and "big Q" are three orders of magnitude apart — misreading them is the classic screenshot mistake.
Combat & Stats
eHP
Effective HP — how much punishment your tower actually survives once health, Death Wave healing, wall, recovery, Chrono Field slow and defense are multiplied together. The standard way to compare defensive builds.
eDamage
Effective damage — your real damage output combining bullet damage (with crits) and Ultimate Weapon damage as separate, additive streams. At high waves the UW stream usually dominates.
Crit / Super Crit
Chance-based damage multipliers on your projectiles. Super crits stack on top of crits and are a core scaling axis for bullet-damage builds.
Multishot
A chance for the tower to fire at several targets at once. More projectiles also means more on-hit procs — one of the quiet multipliers behind Chain Lightning builds.
Rapid Fire
A chance to temporarily fire in a rapid burst. Like all attack-speed effects, its hidden value is more procs per second, not just more bullets.
Bounce Shot
Projectiles can jump to additional enemies after a hit — chance, range and target count are separate upgrades. The Astral Deliverance module builds an entire archetype around it.
Damage / Meter (DPM)
Bonus damage based on how far a projectile has traveled. Nearly worthless on its own — but with Spotlight+ it becomes one of the better damage labs. The textbook "conditional priority".
Defense % vs Defense Absolute
Defense % multiplies all incoming damage down and stays useful forever. Defense Absolute subtracts a flat amount per hit — it falls off hard at high waves because it's additive in a multiplicative system.
Wall
A destructible barrier in front of your tower. It buys time, blocks hits and (with thorns) reflects percentage damage — one of the strongest defensive lines in the game.
Thorns
Reflects a percentage of an attacker's health as damage when they hit your wall or tower. Because it's percentage-based, it scales automatically with enemy health — which grows much faster than enemy damage.
Orbs
Projectiles that circle the tower and shred whatever walks into them. The backbone of the Blender playstyle — and deliberately skipped by Orbless builds that want enemies alive inside coin windows.
Land Mines
Workshop mines that arm around the tower and detonate on contact. Their chance, damage and radius are separate upgrades; the AoE card and Inner Land Mines UW build on the theme.
Shockwave
A periodic blast around the tower that damages and pushes nearby enemies. The Anti-Cube Portal module turns its hits into a damage amplifier.
Knockback
Pushes enemies away on hit. Underrated crowd control: distance bought is time bought, and time is damage.
Death Defy
A chance to survive a killing blow instead of dying. One of the key labs/cards for pushing deep waves.
Crowd Control (CC)
Everything that slows, stuns, knocks back or pulls enemies. CC is a hidden damage multiplier: enemies that stay in range longer eat more bullets, procs and UW ticks.
Shock
A debuff (from Chain Lightning's lab) that makes affected enemies take increased damage — it multiplies your whole damage stack, which is why CL-centric builds dominate.
Coin Bonus
The umbrella multiplier on coins earned, fed by many sources (workshop, GT, BH lab, modules, perks). The in-game "All Coins Bonuses" menu shows the full breakdown.
Free Upgrades
Chance for in-run upgrades to cost nothing. Beyond the economy, some module effects (like Black Hole Digestor) reward stacking free upgrades.
Wave Skip
Chance to skip a wave entirely while keeping rewards flowing. Compounds with everything else that scales per wave — a deceptively strong econ stat.
Enemy Level Skip (ELS)
Chance that the enemy level doesn't increase on a new wave. Slows enemy scaling relative to your run length — a big lever for pushing. Exists twice: as a utility upgrade and again in Workshop Enhancements (ELS+).
Heat-up
Enemies that repeatedly hit your wall or tower ramp up their damage per hit (~4% per hit). The reason a stable frontline eventually breaks if nothing dies.
Recovery Packages
Health pickups that drop during runs and heal the tower. Amount, drop chance and maximum are separate stats; the guardian's Ally chip can heal past the normal cap.
Game Speed
The in-run speed multiplier. Note the displayed speed and the real simulation speed can drift apart on weaker devices — the community has infographics measuring exactly that.
Ultimate Weapons
Ultimate Weapon (UW)
The nine stone-powered weapons — Golden Tower, Black Hole, Death Wave, Chain Lightning, Chrono Field, Smart Missiles, Poison Swamp, Inner Land Mines, Spotlight. Each has stone upgrades, labs and a plus-ability (UW+).
UW+
The upgraded second ability of each Ultimate Weapon (e.g. Golden Combo for GT, Chrono Loop for CF), unlocked with stones after the base UW is developed.
Golden Tower (GT)
The econ backbone: multiplies coin income for its duration. Nearly every farming build is planned around GT uptime and syncing it with Black Hole.
Golden Combo (GT+)
Golden Tower's plus ability — extends the golden window as you keep killing during it. The efficient GT+/GT-duration path is a community infographic classic.
Black Hole (BH)
Pulls enemies into a small area — massive CC and, with its coin-bonus lab, a core econ tool. Perma-BH (100% uptime) is a defining milestone for farming builds.
BH/GT Sync
Aligning Black Hole and Golden Tower so their active windows overlap: enemies get pulled together and killed inside the coin-multiplier window. Desynced UWs waste most of their value.
Death Wave (DW)
A periodic wave that damages (and with labs, heals from) everything on screen. Doubles as offense and a defensive heartbeat in many builds.
Chain Lightning (CL)
Chance-on-hit lightning that arcs between enemies. With the Shock lab it's the strongest damage-side UW in the current meta — instant hit, no travel time, multiplies everything.
Chrono Field (CF)
Slows enemies inside its field — pure CC that also feeds your eHP by reducing incoming pressure. Perma-CF is the defensive equivalent of perma-BH.
Chrono Loop (CF+)
Chrono Field's plus ability — adds a rotating field with an additional hidden slow component that the community only mapped through testing.
Smart Missiles (SM)
Homing missiles with flat damage. Weak in the current meta: raw numbers can't keep pace with exponential enemy health, unlike percentage damage.
Poison Swamp (PS)
Damage-over-time pools under enemies. Percentage-flavored area damage that shines with size and uptime investment.
Inner Land Mines (ILM)
Mines that arm close to the tower and stun what they hit. The stun (time-in-range) is worth more than the damage.
Spotlight (SL)
A cone that multiplies damage dealt to enemies inside it. Positioning-dependent, but the multiplier applies to your entire damage stack — and SL+ is what makes Damage/Meter worth taking.
UW stone upgrades
Each UW levels a small set of stats with stones (duration, cooldown, size, damage, chance, quantity — depending on the weapon). Cooldown and duration are usually the uptime levers that matter first.
UW substats
Core-module substat rolls that push UW stats past their stone caps — extra duration, reduced cooldown and friends. The final ingredient in most perma-uptime math.
Perma (BH / GT / CF)
Shorthand for 100% uptime on an Ultimate Weapon — duration meets cooldown, usually via stones + substats + Multiverse Nexus. Each perma milestone reshapes how a build plays.
Enemies
Basic
The filler enemy: no special traits, minimal reward. Exists to feed your orbs and procs.
Fast
Sprints ahead of the pack. Fasts are why pure slow-and-stall setups still need something that snipes.
Tank
Slow, oversized health pool. Tanks are where percentage damage (thorns, BH damage) visibly outclasses flat damage.
Ranged
Stops at distance and shoots the tower. The reason "kill it before it stops walking" is a defensive stat — and BH has a lab to shut them down entirely.
Boss
The big one at wave's end: massive health, real damage. Boss health/attack reduction labs and the boss trade-off perk exist purely for them.
Protector
Shields enemies around it until it dies. Kill priority number one — everything behind it is temporarily unkillable.
Ray
Elite that locks onto the tower with a beam. Drops cells; on kill-priority lists it sits high because the beam ignores your frontline.
Scatter
Elite that splits into smaller enemies when killed. Drops cells — and briefly multiplies the crowd, which proc-based builds actually enjoy.
Vampire
The sustain elite — it regenerates and punishes slow kill speed. Drops cells; burst damage handles it, chip damage doesn't.
Elites
Rays, scatters and vampires — tougher spawns that drop cells. Elite spawn rate rises with tier, which is why higher tiers fund faster labs.
Fleet
Late-game grouped formations that arrive as one package, with their own escort roles and crowd-control resistance. The reason "one big stun" stops being a full answer.
Mass
Enemies still alive grow stronger the longer they live (~4% per wave alive). Letting a boss walk for ten waves is how runs die.
Workshop & Perks
Workshop
The permanent coin-powered upgrade tree (Attack / Defense / Utility). Slow, steady, and the foundation everything else multiplies.
Workshop Enhancements
A second layer on top of workshop stats that makes maxed lines keep scaling — including its own version of Enemy Level Skip (ELS+).
Perks
In-run choices offered as your run progresses — standard boosts, UW boosts and trade-offs. Perk order and bans are a real part of build strategy.
Trade-off Perks
High-impact perks with a cost attached. Whether to take one depends entirely on your build — hence all the community infographics.
Coin Trade-off Perk
More coins in exchange for a downside elsewhere. For pure farm runs it's usually a yes; the interesting question is how early to grab it.
Cash Trade-off Perk
Multiplies cash-per-wave but removes kill cash. Great for wave-bonus-heavy setups, poison for kill-econ ones — the archetypal "know your build" perk.
50/50 Damage Trade-off Perk
Halves enemy damage and your own damage at the same time. A defensive lifeline for eHP builds, a dead pick for glass cannons.
Boss Trade-off Perk
Weakens boss health but speeds bosses up. Reads scary, plays fine for most builds — a faster boss just meets your wall sooner.
Perk Bans / Auto-pick
Labs that let you ban perks from the pool, auto-pick, and rank auto-pick priority. Essential for AFK farming — your build chooses perks even when you're asleep.
Cards
Cards
Equippable boosts drawn with gems and leveled with duplicates. Card slots limit how many you can run — choosing the loadout per situation (farm vs tourney) matters.
Card Mastery
A stone-powered upgrade on individual maxed cards that adds a second, stronger effect. Expensive and worth planning — mastery order is a popular community debate.
Card Presets
Saved card loadouts you can flip between — the farm-to-tournament switch in two taps.
Damage / Attack Speed / Critical Chance Cards
The bread-and-butter offense cards — flat multipliers on your core attack stats. Rarely exciting, rarely wrong.
Health / Health Regen / Extra Defense Cards
The defensive mirror image — straight multipliers on survivability for eHP-leaning builds.
Cash / Coins / Critical Coin Cards
The econ cards: more cash, more coins, and bonus coins on crits. Farming staples that pay for everything else.
Range Card
Extends tower range — more time shooting each enemy, better Spotlight geometry, earlier ranged-enemy kills.
Slow Aura Card
A slow field around the tower. Crowd control in card form — everything in range dies a little more thoroughly.
Enemy Balance Card
Shifts the spawn mix away from the swarm and toward fewer, meatier enemies. Loved by builds that profit per-enemy, mastered for even stronger control.
Fortress Card
Wall-focused defense card — the wall playstyle's best friend.
Free Upgrades Card
Raises the free-upgrade chance — economy on its own, and fuel for Black Hole Digestor setups.
Extra Orb Card
Adds another orb circling the tower. For Blender builds this is raw kill throughput.
Plasma Cannon Card
Empowers wall thorns damage — the card that turns a wall from a shield into a weapon. Its mastery is a fixture of thorn-build math.
Wave Skip Card
The card version of wave skip. Its mastery is famous for boosting skipped-wave rewards — a quiet farming powerhouse.
Intro Sprint Card
Fast-forwards the early waves of a run. Pure quality of life — the boring part of every run, shortened.
Land Mine Stun Card
Adds a stun to land mine hits. Remember: stun time is damage time — this card is CC dressed as utility.
Recovery Package Chance Card
More healing drops. Combined with the Shatter Shards lab it also feeds daily shard income — a card with two jobs.
Death Ray Card
A beam that periodically burns the strongest thing on screen. Elite and boss insurance in card form.
Energy Net Card
Catches and holds enemies in place. More time-in-range, more procs — the CC theme again.
Super Tower Card
Periodically supercharges the tower's combat stats. One of the flashiest cards in the game — and its mastery decision is a meme-tier debate.
Second Wind Card
A comeback mechanic after your defenses fail — one more chance to stabilize before the run truly ends.
Demon Mode Card
A temporary berserk state with boosted offense at a defensive cost. Timing-dependent and beloved by push builds.
Energy Shield Card
Blocks hits outright at intervals. Mediocre while farming, jumps to elite status in Legend tournaments where single hits actually threaten you.
Wave Accelerator Card
Speeds up enemy spawning. Sounds suicidal, is actually econ: more spawns per minute means more kills per minute — its mastery pushes spawn rates to the cap.
Berserker Card
Converts damage you've absorbed into bonus damage. The tankier you build, the harder it hits — an eHP build's favorite offense card.
Ultimate Crit Card
Lets Ultimate Weapon damage crit. Since UW damage dominates late, this card scales with your whole endgame.
Nuke Card
Periodically wipes the screen. Panic button, boss-wave opener and spawn-wave cleaner in one.
Area of Effect Card
Boosts the game's area-damage sources (mines, swamp, missiles, flame bot). A glue card for AoE-flavored builds.
Modules & Guardian
Modules
Equippable gear in four slots — Cannon (damage), Armor (defense), Generator (econ), Core (UWs). Each has a main effect, rarity, substats and, at high rarity, a unique effect.
Substats
The secondary rolls on a module ("sub-module effects"). Rerolled with shards; ban labs let you exclude the junk rolls from the pool.
Ancestral
The top module rarity, upgradeable through 1–5 stars. Star progress gates the strongest unique effects.
Assist Modules
A late-game milestone feature: extra module slots (unlocked with stones) whose equipped modules contribute a scaled-down version of their effects.
Module Merging
Combining lower-rarity modules to climb the rarity ladder. The requirements get intricate enough that the community made flow diagrams for it.
Astral Deliverance (AD)
Cannon unique built around Bounce Shot: bigger bounce range and projectiles that gain damage with every bounce. The centerpiece of bounce-damage builds.
Being Annihilator (BA)
Cannon unique: after a super crit, your next several shots are guaranteed super crits. Streaky, brutal, and the reason super-crit chance thresholds matter.
Anti-Cube Portal (ACP)
Armor unique: enemies hit by your Shockwave temporarily take amplified damage. Turns a defensive pulse into a damage window.
Sharp Fortitude (SF)
Armor unique for wall builds — sharpens what the wall does back to attackers. The wall-thorns tables comparing it to a standard wall are a community staple.
Black Hole Digestor (BHD)
Generator unique: temporary bonus coin income for every free upgrade received on the current wave. Marries the free-upgrade stat to your econ — and loves Wave Skip's mastery.
Project Funding (PF)
Generator unique that scales damage with the cash you're holding. Suddenly "don't spend everything instantly" is a damage strategy.
Dimension Core (DC)
Core unique that roughly doubles Shock's value — which makes it the natural partner of every Chain Lightning build.
Multiverse Nexus (MVN)
The Core ancestral whose effect periodically re-triggers and aligns your Ultimate Weapons — the engine behind perma BH/GT math. Dedicated community calculators exist just for its sync timing.
Galaxy Compressor (GComp)
Core unique tied to UW cooldown compression — a load-bearing piece in permanent-uptime plans (perma-CF calculators budget around it).
Guardian
The guild companion that sits by your tower and uses chips, powered by bits from guild missions.
Ally Chip
Guardian chip that converts an enemy into a healing package that can push the tower's health past its normal recovery cap.
Attack Chip
Guardian chip that damages nearby enemies based on the damage they've already taken — a finisher, not an opener.
Bounty Chip
Guardian chip (formerly "Steal") that marks elites, bosses and fleets to drop bonus coins on death. Guild-flavored econ.
Fetch Chip
Guardian chip that goes and grabs drops for you. Quality of life with real value for AFK farming — its upgrade path even has a community-made optimization sheet.
Bots
Bots
The four medal-powered drones that orbit your battlefield: Flame, Thunder, Golden and Amplify. Medal placement (and event-timed respecs) decides which carry your build.
Flame Bot
Sweeps an area with burning damage — the offensive workhorse, boosted further by the AoE card.
Thunder Bot
Stuns groups of enemies on its pass. Another entry in the "CC is secretly damage" ledger.
Golden Bot
Marks enemies to be worth bonus coins — an econ multiplier that farming builds sync into their GT/BH windows. Cooldown planning has its own labs, medals and community sheets.
Amplify Bot
Makes enemies inside its zone take extra damage. Multiplies everyone else's work — bots included.
Bot Respec
Resetting invested medals to redistribute them — costs gems, limited per event, discounted through the vault. Plan before you spend.
Labs & Progression
Labs
The coin-powered research system (199 labs and counting) that runs in real time. Lab priority is the most-asked question in the community — speed-ups, discounts and tier lists all orbit it.
Lab Speed-ups
Cell-powered multipliers on lab slots. Because cells come from elites, your farming tier directly controls research speed.
Lab Rushing (gems)
Finishing a lab instantly with gems. Long labs get progressively discounted per hour rushed — the discount table is worth knowing before you spend.
Buy Multiplier
The lab that lets you buy workshop upgrades in bulk. Sounds like comfort, is actually mandatory — late-game numbers make single purchases absurd.
Battle Condition Reduction
The lab that blunts tournament battle conditions by a percentage. One of the quiet "tournament power" investments.
Milestones
Tier/wave checkpoints that unlock features (labs, assist modules) and pay one-time rewards. "Milestone runs" are pushes made specifically to reach the next unlock.
Tier
The difficulty level you start a run at. Higher tiers scale enemies harder but pay better coins, cells and milestones — "what tier should I farm?" is its own genre of tooling.
Vault / Harmony Tree
Quality-of-life upgrade tree (auto-restart, ad-gem stacking, preset unlocks, cooldown sliders). Small effects that compound into serious account speed.
Auto Restart
Vault upgrade that starts the next run automatically when you die. The single most important unlock for overnight farming.
Relics
Permanent stat boosts collected from events. Individually small, collectively a real multiplier.
Events
The rotating limited-time mode with its own missions, currency and shop — the main source of relics, medals, skins and themed rewards.
Event Shop
Where event currency becomes permanent value. Prioritizing relics and long-term unlocks over cosmetics is the standard advice — cosmetics forgive you later.
Tournaments
Tournaments
Twice-weekly brackets of 30 players pushing one run for placement. The main renewable source of stones — league and placement decide the payout.
Leagues
The tournament ladder: Copper → Silver → Gold → Platinum → Champion → Legend. Promotion and relegation depend on placement.
Battle Conditions (BC)
Tournament modifiers that change how runs play. Higher leagues stack more of them; a lab reduces their impact. They also appear on high tiers (14+) outside tournaments.
Armored Enemies (BC)
The battle condition where enemies block your first hits entirely. Attack-speed and multishot builds shrug; slow heavy hitters suffer.
Boss's Ultimate (BC)
The battle condition where bosses spawn overhealed beyond their normal health. Boss insurance (Death Ray, thorns) earns its keep here.
Basic's Ultimate (BC)
The battle condition where basics can spawn as other enemy types. The wave composition you farmed against stops existing.
Bracket luck
Your 30-player bracket is drawn from whoever joins in your window — the same wave count can be rank 4 or rank 14. Tools like thetower.lol's live placement analysis quantify it.
Respec
Rebuilding your setup (workshop respec, bot respec, card swaps) specifically for tournaments, then switching back to farm. Serious players maintain two builds.
Overheal
Health above the normal maximum — bosses get it from their battle condition, your tower can get it from the guardian's Ally chip.
Builds & Community Slang
Glass Cannon (GC)
All-in damage build that skips most defense: kill everything before it reaches you. Spikes hard in tournaments, falls apart when the killing stops — wall rebuild labs exist mostly for this build.
eHP build
The defensive counterpart — stack survivability and let percentage damage (thorns, BH) do the killing. Steady, AFK-friendly, slower spikes.
Hybrid
The middle path: enough eHP to survive, enough eDamage to push. Where most accounts end up between the extremes.
Blender
The classic early strategy: orbs shred everything that gets close while thorns handle the big stuff. Named for what the area around your tower looks like.
Orbless
Builds that deliberately drop orbs so enemies survive to be killed inside coin-multiplier windows (Spotlight, GT/BH) — trading kill speed for income per kill.
Farming run
A run tuned for income (coins/cells) at a tier you comfortably sustain, usually overnight. The opposite of a push.
Push
A run tuned to reach the highest possible wave or the next milestone, usually with a tournament-style setup.
AFK-friendly
A setup that survives and earns without being watched — auto-pick perks, auto-restart, stable eHP. What most farming builds optimize for.
Creator code
A code entered in the in-game shop that credits a content creator with supporting their channel — at no cost to the player. Ours is in the footer.
Whale
A player who buys the monthly stone packs at scale. Because bought stones compound through econ just like earned ones, stones-per-year is the community's informal whale detector.
Theorycrafting
The community sport of modeling the game's math — uptime formulas, eHP spreadsheets, RoI paths. Half the links on our resources page exist because of it.
